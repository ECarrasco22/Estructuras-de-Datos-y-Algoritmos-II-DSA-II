## 📚 Topics and Projects

The course covered several algorithmic techniques and practical programming challenges, including:

- **Divide and Conquer:** Solving problems by breaking them down into smaller subproblems.
- **Brute Force:** Exploring possible solutions through exhaustive evaluation of alternatives.
- **Password Attack (Brute Force):** An academic presentation exploring the brute-force approach in the context of password attacks.
- **Backtracking:** Finding solutions by exploring different possibilities and retracing steps when a path does not lead to a valid solution.
- **Knight's Tour:** Solving the chessboard traversal problem by finding a sequence of knight moves that visits the required squares.
- **Greedy Algorithms:** Building solutions through locally optimal decisions.
- **Weighted Graphs:** Working with graphs whose edges contain associated weights or costs.
- **Deck of Cards:** A programming exercise involving the representation and manipulation of a deck of cards.
- **Knapsack Problem:** Exploring an optimization problem involving the selection of items under a limited capacity.
- **Number to Words:** Implementing a solution to convert numerical values into their written representation.
- **Tic-Tac-Toe:** Implementation of the classic Tic-Tac-Toe game.
- **Tic-Tac-Toe with Minimax AI:** Applying the Minimax algorithm to develop an AI opponent capable of evaluating possible moves and making strategic decisions.

## 🧩 Final Laboratory Project: Maze Solver

The final laboratory project consists of a **Maze Solver** application developed in Python.

The application uses the **Backtracking algorithm** to find a valid path through a maze. It explores different routes, retracing its steps whenever it encounters a dead end or an invalid path.

The project includes a graphical interface that allows users to visualize the maze and observe the algorithm's exploration and backtracking process.
